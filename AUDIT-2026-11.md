Claude's independent review established that **Phase 1 (`phase-1-online-only`, commit `2e925ef`) exists on origin but was never merged into `main`**. The current `main` tip is `87629c5`, the Phase-0 merge.

This changes the interpretation of the original audit: the localStorage persistence and stale-browser fallback findings are valid against `main` because the Phase 1 changes never shipped—not because Phase 1 was subsequently rolled back.

**Required sequencing decision:** merge and validate Phase 1 before proceeding with PR-B's schema expansion or onboarding work. Review the Phase 1 diff and its deploy implications; do not assume its branch is production-ready merely because it exists. Preserve BNFODIO and all existing results, payments, attendance, and Hifz/Tahfiz data throughout the merge and deployment.

## Executive summary

The current `main` baseline still contains legacy browser persistence and client-side school credentials. Claude's review also identified a deeper privacy issue: plaintext teacher/admin passwords are embedded in the shared school JSON row, and the overly broad `__master__` read policy exposes platform-wide PIN/batch information. Restore can report success without a verified durable commit.

The immediate plan is deliberately narrower than the original audit's blocker list:

- Merge Phase 1 to `main` as the prerequisite.
- Deliver the `__master__` access correction and PIN/batch isolation, plus safe restore/CAS handling, together in PR-B.
- Move teacher/admin credentials to Supabase Auth and remove in-app password displays/reset flows in PR-C.

Other issues remain recorded and scheduled, but do not expand PR-A or silently widen PR-B.

## Severity-ranked findings

### P0 — Critical: plaintext school-user credentials are embedded in the shared school data row

**Evidence:** `schoolLogin()` compares submitted passwords with `DB.users` values at approximately `index.html:3097–3100`. More importantly, `DB.users[].password` is part of the school's `school_data.data` JSON document. Claude traced writes of these values to approximately `index.html:3250`, `3314`, `7218`, and `7862`. The owner UI display around line 3238 is a symptom; the underlying issue is that credentials are persisted in the shared application data itself.

**Impact:** Any authenticated member who can read their school's broad `school_data` row can obtain every colleague's plaintext password in that school's JSON. Client-side comparison is not an authentication boundary. This creates direct account-compromise and credential-reuse risk.

**Joint assessment:** GPT-6's original audit identified plaintext credentials and the owner display, but understated the exposure by treating the display as the primary evidence. Claude's deeper data-path analysis is accepted. This is a critical security issue.

**Required remediation / sequencing:** Teacher/admin passwords must move to Supabase Auth; remove password fields from application data and all UI displays; use email-based recovery/reset rather than owner-visible or owner-entered passwords. This credential migration and removal of owner password/reset screens are PR-C scope. Plan safe credential migration and rotation; never log or expose plaintext passwords.

### P1 — Critical: `__master__` RLS exposes platform-wide PINs, batches, and related data

**Evidence:** `supabase/migrations/0001_init.sql:102–110` includes `id = '__master__'` as an OR condition in the authenticated read policy. Claude confirms that every authenticated user can therefore read the `__master__` row. `MASTER.pins[]` and `MASTER.batches` include parent/pupil result-checking PINs, including unused codes and prices.

**Impact:** A teacher at school A may enumerate PINs and related commercial data belonging to school B. This is a cross-tenant privacy and revenue leak. It is more serious than the original audit's general concern that the master row exposed owner/admin metadata.

**Joint assessment:** GPT-6's original audit noticed the broad `__master__` policy but missed the sensitive PIN/batch contents and cross-tenant impact. Claude's finding is accepted and elevated as a concrete P1 exposure.

**Required fix:** Restrict full `__master__` reads to the exact owner role. Authenticated non-owner users must receive only a deliberately stripped, safe platform projection (for example platform name and minimum-version metadata), not the underlying master JSON. Move `MASTER.pins` and `MASTER.batches` into dedicated per-school tables with RLS keyed to memberships. Test anon, owner, and users from different schools. Do not expose PINs or prices across tenants.

### P1 — High: restore can report success without verifying the database commit

**Evidence:** `doRestore()` around `index.html:1011–1014` performs a direct upsert instead of using the normal CAS path. The error handling can swallow a failure and the success toast is unconditional.

**Impact:** A failed restore may be presented as successful, leaving the operator with false confidence that important school data was restored. The direct upsert also bypasses the application's concurrency protection.

**Joint assessment:** Confirmed by both reviewers. The fix must cover durable acknowledgement, not merely display a returned error.

**Required fix:** Route restore through `_commitRowCAS` (or a rigorously equivalent validated commit path); await the operation, check returned errors, and verify the committed revision before showing success. Keep a recoverable pre-restore snapshot and require explicit confirmation. Test failed writes, conflicts, and successful verified restores.

### P1 — High: broad school-data row access is not sufficiently role-scoped

**Evidence:** The reviewed RLS policy permits a school member to select the school's `school_data` row, a broad JSON document containing pupils, staff, results, finance, attendance, Hifz and related records.

**Impact:** A low-privilege member may read substantially more information than their role requires. This is an architectural limitation of putting many sensitive domains in one row and authorizing access only at the tenant-row level.

**Joint assessment:** Both reviewers agree this is a real design risk. It remains P1 as an authorization limitation, but it is distinct from the specific, immediately actionable `__master__` PIN exposure above. Do not claim that tenant membership alone provides record-level privacy.

**Required action:** Before broad onboarding or adding more roles, define and test role/record-level authorization. Plan incremental normalization and narrowly scoped RPC/API operations for sensitive domains. Do not turn this audit into an unplanned big-bang migration.

### P2 — High: legacy localStorage persistence and stale fallback remain on current main

**Evidence:** `loadStore`, `saveStoreLocal`, `loadBase`, `saveBase`, and `backupSnapshot` use localStorage around `index.html:846–880`; `boot()` invokes local loading; `pullRow()` can return `STORE[id]` after a failed request; write paths call local persistence/backup helpers.

**Impact:** This contradicts the intended cloud-only Phase 1 architecture. A network failure can fall back to stale local data, and browser backups may retain school records on shared devices.

**Joint assessment:** Confirmed as a valid finding on `main`. The meta-finding above explains why it remains present: Phase 1 was never merged. It is not evidence of a Phase 1 rollback.

**Required action:** Merge Phase 1 to `main` first, after reviewing its diff and tests. Verify cloud failure is visible and cannot silently treat stale browser data as authoritative. Add regression tests proving school data is not mirrored to localStorage.

### P2 — Cosmetic/noisy: anonymous owner bootstrap ordering

**Evidence:** `boot()` calls `cloudPull('__master__')` before rendering the owner sign-in UI. The RLS-filtered read uses `.maybeSingle()`; for an anonymous user denied by RLS, Supabase returns no visible row (`data: null`) without necessarily returning an error. The flow then falls through to `seedMaster()` and renders the login UI.

**Impact:** Claude reports that owner sign-in remains reachable and empirically works. The original GPT-6 audit incorrectly treated this as a P0 authentication deadlock. The observed side effects are cosmetic (a seeded platform name may briefly appear before login) and noisy (a denied `saveSchoolRow('DEMO')` write may occur on anonymous landings).

**Joint assessment:** GPT-6 accepts Claude's evidence and downgrades this from P0 to P2. The original conclusion overstated the impact because it assumed the filtered read would take the error branch.

**Recommendation:** Clean up anonymous boot behavior and avoid unnecessary denied writes, but do not block PR-B on the cosmetic bootstrap issue. Retest the fresh-session sign-in flow after the Phase 1 merge.

### P2 — Medium: CAS does not protect first insert from concurrent writers

**Evidence:** `_commitRowCAS()` protects updates using `updated_at`, but when a row is absent it uses an upsert path without an atomic insert-if-absent condition.

**Impact:** Concurrent first writers could race while creating a brand-new tenant row.

**Joint assessment:** GPT-6 accepts Claude's downgrade from P1 to P2. The practical exposure is limited to first-ever creation of a new tenant, which should be owned by the onboarding RPC in PR-C; it is not identified as a current BNFODIO risk.

**Recommendation:** Ensure tenant creation is atomic in the onboarding flow. Add concurrent creation tests there. Do not treat this as a reason to expand PR-B beyond its agreed restore and master-data scope.

### P2 — Medium: `sessionStorage` auto-login is client-side identity state

**Evidence:** School login writes the logged-in user ID to `sessionStorage` key `sms_su` around `index.html:3101`; boot around `index.html:5636` reads it and re-identifies the user by looking up the ID in `DB.users`.

**Impact:** This is not a Supabase Auth session. It relies on client-side application data to re-establish identity and must not be mistaken for authenticated authorization.

**Joint assessment:** Missed in GPT-6's original audit; Claude's finding is accepted. The risk is addressed as part of the teacher/admin Supabase Auth migration rather than as a standalone PR-B change.

**Required action:** Remove this legacy auto-login mechanism in PR-C when school users move to Supabase Auth. Confirm logout clears the real Auth session and test session expiry/revocation.

### P3 — Low: every logged-in user may attempt an unnecessary `saveMaster()` write on boot

**Evidence:** Claude identifies the APP_VERSION/minVersion bump path around `index.html:5621` as causing a `saveMaster()` attempt on each logged-in user's boot. RLS denies non-owner writes.

**Impact:** The policy protects the row, but repeated denied writes add avoidable Supabase log noise and unnecessary requests.

**Joint assessment:** Missed in GPT-6's original audit; accepted as P3 based on the reported RLS enforcement.

**Recommendation:** Gate master-version writes to the exact owner role and avoid the request for ordinary users.

### P2 — Medium: single JSONB document per school creates scaling and contention risks

**Evidence:** Most school state is stored in one `school_data.data` document. Owner boot uses `pullAllStrict()` to select `id,data` for all rows.

**Impact:** Small changes can rewrite a large document; concurrent users contend on one row; owner startup cost grows with tenant data; role-scoped access is difficult.

**Recommendation:** Plan incremental normalization by domain. Do not perform a big-bang migration. Start with high-sensitivity/high-write domains and retain compatibility adapters with reconciliation and rollback.

### P2 — Medium: external scripts are not pinned with integrity metadata

**Evidence:** External CDN scripts are loaded from the document head; Supabase JS uses a floating `@2` version and Chart.js appears more than once in the reviewed page.

**Impact:** Supply-chain changes can alter runtime behavior; duplicate scripts increase payload and potential conflicts.

**Recommendation:** Pin exact tested versions, add SRI/crossorigin where supported, remove duplicates, and document an update process.

### P2 — Medium: extensive `innerHTML` usage needs sink-by-sink review

**Evidence:** Static scan found approximately 228 `innerHTML` occurrences. An `esc()` helper exists around line 1167, but this scan did not establish that every dynamic HTML, URL, attribute, style, or event-handler sink is safe.

**Impact:** Unescaped user-controlled content can lead to stored or reflected XSS.

**Recommendation:** Trace each dynamic value to its source; prefer `textContent` and DOM construction. Sanitize permitted rich text with a maintained allowlist sanitizer. Validate URL protocols and attribute contexts. Add adversarial tests. This audit does not claim a confirmed exploit.

### P2 — Medium: service-worker update routine has broad cache side effects

**Evidence:** `sw.js` is intentionally transparent/no-cache, but `forceUpdate()` unregisters service workers and clears all Cache Storage for the origin around `index.html:5593–5594`.

**Impact:** Clearing every origin cache can affect unrelated applications hosted on the same origin.

**Recommendation:** Scope cleanup to this application's own cache namespace and service-worker registrations; do not clear origin-wide caches.

### P2 — Medium: accessibility and dark-mode coverage are not demonstrated

**Evidence:** The page has responsive breakpoints, but the reviewed source does not demonstrate complete dark-mode support, consistent visible focus, screen-reader status announcements, or systematic keyboard navigation across all workflows.

**Recommendation:** Validate WCAG 2.2 AA (or the agreed AA baseline) using automated and manual testing. Include keyboard-only workflows, focus management, contrast, labels, error announcements, touch targets, reduced motion, and dark-mode contrast.

### P3 — Medium: owner role check should fail closed

**Evidence:** Owner login check around `index.html:3080` uses a conditional equivalent to `if(role && role!=='owner')`, which rejects a wrong non-empty role but does not reject missing role metadata.

**Impact:** Missing role metadata may not be rejected at this client-side gate.

**Joint assessment:** GPT-6 accepts Claude's downgrade from P1 to P3. Claude's review notes that RLS currently enforces the owner role server-side and the existing owner Auth user has the expected app_metadata. This remains worthwhile defense-in-depth, not a current blocker.

**Recommendation:** Require `app_metadata.role === 'owner'` exactly in the UI guard and retain server-side enforcement in RLS.

### P3 — Medium: audit logging exists as schema but operational coverage is unclear

**Evidence:** `audit_log` is created in the initial migration, but the reviewed source does not demonstrate a complete, tamper-resistant event-writing strategy for sensitive operations.

**Recommendation:** Define server-side audit events for auth/role changes, restore, exports, billing, sensitive record access, and administrative actions. Avoid logging secrets or unnecessary student data.

### P3 — Medium: automated regression coverage is not evidenced

**Recommendation:** Establish repeatable tests for authentication/RLS, CAS, restore, merge3, marks/results calculations, attendance, fees/payments, Hifz/Tahfiz scoring, 604-page Mushaf mapping, and coverage application before major UI or schema changes.

## Revised must-fix sequence before PR-B

This replaces the original nine-item blocker list and reflects the joint review.

1. **Merge Phase 1 to `main`.** Phase 1 is currently an unmerged branch (`phase-1-online-only`, commit `2e925ef`); review its diff, test it, and confirm the cloud-only behavior before building on it.
2. **Move teacher/admin passwords out of `DB.users` and into Supabase Auth.** This is PR-C scope; do not onboard more users while plaintext credentials remain embedded in school JSON.
3. **Tighten `__master__` access and isolate PIN/batch data.** Owner reads the full master row; authenticated non-owners receive only a stripped safe projection. Move `MASTER.pins` and `MASTER.batches` into dedicated per-school tables with membership-keyed RLS. This is PR-B scope.
4. **Fix restore durability and concurrency.** Await the commit, check errors, verify the committed row/revision before success, and route through `_commitRowCAS` rather than a direct upsert. This is PR-B scope.
5. **Remove password displays and the in-app “Reset password” flow** from owner Schools and Manage-School screens. Once Supabase Auth owns credentials, resets are email-based, never typed or displayed in-app. This is PR-C scope.

The five items above are the agreed release sequence, not permission to skip tests or BNFODIO safety checks. The broad school-row role-scoping limitation remains P1 and must be addressed in the security design before broad onboarding; it must not be represented as solved merely by fixing `__master__`.
